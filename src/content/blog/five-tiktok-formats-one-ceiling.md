For two weeks in late October and early November, [OutfitMaker](https://outfitmaker.ai) posted short videos on TikTok and Instagram almost every evening. The goal was simple: find a format that holds attention long enough for the algorithm to push it past its first test batch.

I changed one thing at a time and wrote every number down. This is what the data said.

## The setup

OutfitMaker turns photos of the clothes you already own into outfits. The TikTok account targets the UK, where most viewers already were. Every video followed the same rule: the exact search phrase people type (from TikTok's "content gap" insights) on screen in the first second and in the caption.

The benchmark was the best video before the test: a 7 second "which outfit?" duel that held viewers for 3.6 seconds on average, with 6.1% watching to the end.

## Five formats, side by side

| Format | Length | Views | Avg. watch time | Watched to the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. AI duel, two looks side by side (benchmark) | 7 s | about 770 | 3.6 s | 6.1% |
| 2. Same duel, longer, with a "comment 1 or 2" hook | 10 s | 744 | 2.5 s | 1.96% |
| 3. Same idea in a bright "game show" style | 8.5 s | 768 | 2.4 s | 2.28% |
| 4. Real screen recording of the app, "POV" hook | 9.8 s | 734 | 2.4 s | 0.69% |
| 5. Finished look full screen at 0:00, with a voice | 10.2 s | 277 (day 1) | 2.5 s | 1.43% |

Three things stand out.

**The audience was never the problem.** Across the tests, women made up 66 to 81% of viewers and 69 to 87% were in the UK. On the videos where TikTok showed the full age split, 83 to 93% of viewers were 35 or older. That is exactly who I wanted to reach.

**The ceiling did not move.** The first four formats landed between 734 and about 770 views, roughly the size of TikTok's first test batch for a small account, and the fifth was at 277 after one day. None earned a second push.

**Viewers left at the same moment.** Whatever I changed (length, colours, a real app recording, a voice, the result shown first), most people swiped away at about two seconds. Engagement stayed near zero: one like on the last video, no comments, no shares. The "comment 1 or 2 and we will style the winner tomorrow" mechanic got zero comments on 744 views, so I dropped it.

## What social did not bring

From 20 October to 9 November, organic TikTok sent one visit to the site and Instagram none, against 8,880 from Google. Every paying customer and trial I could trace since September came from Google search.

A small paid TikTok test brought 93 visits for 21 € and no sign-ups at all. Part of that was a bug: TikTok's in-app browser blocks Google sign-in, and that button was first on the page. I [wrote about that one separately](/blog/93-paid-clicks-zero-signups-google-button-tiktok). Fixing it did not change the bigger picture.

Search was quietly doing the work: 10,031 clicks from Google in 28 days, up 27% on the previous period.

## The lesson

Short video is a slot machine with a two-second handle. The formats were reasonable, the targeting was right, and the data was consistent: watching an AI dress someone else does not stop the thumb.

So I stopped producing. The account keeps a light presence (one or two posts a week, no heavy production), and the time goes where customers actually come from: pages that answer what people search for, and what happens after they land.

The one variable I never tested is a real person talking to camera. If I try anything else on TikTok, it will be that, and it costs a phone and ten seconds.
